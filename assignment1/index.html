1. Flatness

From inside the cave...

I was walking peacefully through the forest, enjoying the fresh air and the quiet sound of leaves moving in the wind. The sunlight was soft, and everything felt calm. I had no destination in mind. I was simply walking, breathing, and appreciating the stillness of nature. The trees were tall and thick, and birds were singing somewhere above me. It was the kind of day that makes you forget about everything else. While I was walking deeper into the forest, I noticed something unusual. Behind a group of large trees covered in vines and leaves, there was a dark opening. It was almost completely hidden by branches and plants. If I had not looked carefully, I would have missed it. As I moved closer, I realized it was a cave. The entrance was surrounded by thick trees that seemed to protect it from the outside world. Curiosity immediately took control of me. I have always been a curious person. If I see something mysterious, I cannot ignore it. I need to understand it. So I pushed the branches aside and stepped inside the cave. The air inside was cooler and heavier. The light from outside slowly faded behind me. As my eyes adjusted to the darkness, I noticed something surprising. There were four different entrances inside the cave. Four separate paths. It felt like some kind of puzzle or challenge. I wanted to explore all of them. I chose the first entrance and walked inside carefully. After a few steps, I found myself standing in front of a massive stone block. It was large and smooth, like a wall carved out of the cave itself. On the surface of that stone, there was a drawing. A single face. The face had no real features. It was simple. Just two eyes and a straight mouth. (The people inside the cave believe that this face is real. They do not question where it comes from. Because they only see the wall in front of them, they assume that what appears on the surface is the full truth. They have never seen anything beyond it). The mouth was not smiling and not frowning. It was completely neutral. The eyes were empty circles. It did not look friendly, but it did not look angry either. It just existed there, silently. I felt afraid at first. The cave was quiet. Too quiet. But my curiosity was stronger than my fear. Next to the stone wall, I noticed four separate rocks placed in a row. They looked almost identical, except one of them was slightly sticking out from the wall. It looked like it could be pressed. My curiosity took control again. I decided to press the stone. The moment I pressed it, something unexpected happened. The eyes on the stone wall began to move. They slowly shifted and the eyes slowly began to move close and far from each other. (At this moment, I began to understand what Flatness means. Flatness is when a person sees only one perspective and believes it is complete reality. The people inside the cave accept the surface of the wall as truth. They do not imagine that something beyond the wall could be causing what they see.) It was not a fast movement, but it was enough to make my heart race. The face was no longer completely still. It felt alive. My curiosity, however, was not satisfied. I wanted to understand more. So I decided to explore the second entrance. Just like the first one, I found another large stone wall with the same face drawn on it. Again, there were four stones next to it. And once again, one of them was slightly sticking out. This time, it was the second stone. I began to think that this was some kind of puzzle. Maybe if I solved it, I would find treasure. Maybe there was something hidden behind the wall. I pressed the second stone carefully. The mouth on the face began to move upward. The neutral expression changed into something strange. It was not what I was expecting. The face felt more aware. More expressive.

At this point, I was both scared and excited. The cave was reacting to me. Every action I took caused a response. (Their worldview is flat because it is limited to a single surface. Without questioning the source of the images, they remain trapped in one-dimensional thinking.) I moved to the third entrance. The same wall. The same face. The same four stones. And again, only one stone was sticking out. But this time I saw the whole face clearly. It was reflecting a shadow and seemed to float in the air. It felt magical. I felt like I was watching everything from another point of view, as if I were standing outside the cave.. I pressed it without hesitation..

2. Seeing Double

From outside the cave...

This time, the entire face began to move forward. It slowly pushed outward from the cave wall the face slowly moved forward from the wall. It was no longer flat against the surface. It entered the space in front of it. It felt three-dimensional. It felt real. I could see shadows forming behind it. Now I understood that this cave was not random. It was designed to test perception. What seemed flat was not truly flat. What seemed still was capable of movement. Finally, I entered the fourth entrance. My heart was beating fast. I already knew what to expect. Another stone wall, another face, another stone waiting to be pressed. When I pressed the final stone, the cave itself began to change. There was a light from outside the cave. I saw the light source slowly move, changing the shadows and making them shift across the wall..

Shadows stretched across the wall in new directions. The entire atmosphere became dramatic. The face looked different. More intense. More alive. (The movement of objects outside the cave, combined with the light source, is what causes the shadows to appear inside. The people inside mistake these shadows for reality because they cannot see the original objects creating them. Seeing Double means holding two perspectives at once. It means understanding what the people inside see while also knowing what is truly happening outside the cave. It requires stepping back and recognizing that reality has more than one dimension.)

Standing there, I realized something important. The cave was not about treasure. It was about perspective. From close up, the face seemed flat and harmless. With interaction, it came alive. With distance and light, it gained depth. I entered the cave as a curious person looking for adventure. I left it understanding something deeper. Reality changes depending on how you look at it. What appears simple may hide movement. What appears flat may hide depth. And sometimes, curiosity is the only way to reveal what is truly there.
